# Quest 3S infrared camera investigation

Research reviewed 2026-11-14. These sources establish a promising rooted-device research route, not a tested night-vision implementation in Lumen Mesh. This repository does not root a headset or access IR images.

## Strongest built-in camera lead: QuestLHSync

[QuestLHSync](https://github.com/CreoleVR/QuestLHSync) says it supports Quest Pro, Quest 3 and Quest 3S with Magisk root. The [Quest implementation](https://github.com/CreoleVR/QuestLHSync/blob/b0c251d6810a30ef6d47d71c51af66d62fbb55cd/magisk/src/quest.c) uses Frida to run a reader inside the Oculus sensors HAL and obtains camera calibration from the headset. The [reader](https://github.com/CreoleVR/QuestLHSync/blob/b0c251d6810a30ef6d47d71c51af66d62fbb55cd/magisk/src/lhsight.js) looks for camera-sized shared DMA buffer mappings in that process; its companion [C code](https://github.com/CreoleVR/QuestLHSync/blob/b0c251d6810a30ef6d47d71c51af66d62fbb55cd/magisk/src/lhsight.c) reads pixel data to detect bright spots. The code uses calibration and makes assumptions about image dimensions, buffer layout and allocation order. Its 640x480 side-camera comment describes Quest Pro and Quest 3; do not assume a particular Quest 3S sensor or resolution without checking its calibration.

This is materially stronger evidence than just an idea: its code reads image data locally. However, QuestLHSync deliberately transmits **spot positions, not images**, and is designed for tracking SteamVR base stations, not displaying a scene. A modified reader and local image transport could theoretically expose frames for a viewer. No such adaptation was built or tested here. Camera selection, buffer layout, image quality and latency, alternating exposure, frame synchronization, field of view, headset alignment, and operation under 3S IR illumination are all unresolved. The project says cameras must be active for tracking and buffer discovery can fail on an unsupported device/OS build. Root, system-service instrumentation, and private buffer layout are unsupported, firmware-dependent, and can affect device stability or security. Do not expose an unauthenticated image stream.

## Root availability is firmware-specific

[FreeXR's exploit index](https://github.com/FreeXR/exploits) catalogs multiple temporary-root research projects. Its older [CVE-2025-21479 proof of concept](https://github.com/zhuowei/cheese) lists Quest 3S v79 build 117688.9900.610 and earlier within a stated range; this does **not** mean later firmware is impossible to root. The index also describes later exploit families and lists exact supported builds, including 3S build 3814840024700610 for one route at the time reviewed. These are third-party claims, may change, and must be checked against the exact headset build and exploit project. Having developer mode or sideloading enabled does not itself provide root. Exploitation can disrupt the headset, reduce security, and in the worst case make recovery difficult. No rooting has been attempted.

## Supported access and other routes

- Meta's [Camera2 passthrough API](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/native/android/pca-native-documentation/) documents forward-facing **RGB** camera access with permission. It does not document raw IR tracking-camera access. Brightness processing cannot recover details the RGB sensor did not capture in darkness.
- [TrackerBridge](https://github.com/celesrabbit-lab/TrackerBridge) reports working external USB camera input on a Quest 3S, sending frames to a PC for face/eye tracking. This supports the feasibility of an external-camera input route, but does not demonstrate Lumen Mesh night vision or access to built-in IR cameras. A suitable IR-sensitive camera, illumination, local display implementation and calibration would still be needed.
- [Quest3-IRFusion](https://github.com/A31A18B25C9D012/Quest3-IRFusion) describes an external IR/thermal-camera approach with a privileged service and custom Android build. Its claims and hardware compatibility are not verified here; it is not evidence of a stock Quest APK that works.
- [Quest Pro Camera Network Relay](https://github.com/MenekoByte/Quest-Pro-Camera-Network-Relay) describes rooted streaming of the Pro's **inward-facing** cameras. The 3S does not have those cameras, so it is not a direct solution for this headset.
- The Quest 3S has IR illuminators for tracking. Low-light tracking may work, depending on environment and software, but does not grant the app camera frames. Do not assume the illuminators remain on or adequately illuminate a scene for an external camera.

## Next experiment if a compatible rooted 3S is available

First establish the exact model, Horizon OS build and root status; verify QuestLHSync's documented support for that build. With the owner's consent and a controlled, well-lit stationary test, characterize one local tracking-camera frame, validate camera identity, format and exposure, and measure whether the existing reader can copy it without disrupting tracking. Only after that would a local-only preview and headset alignment be worth implementing. Test darkness separately with the headset stationary; an IR scene view would not prove safe navigation. No steps in this paragraph have been performed.
